Her Story

Rani Lakshmibai was born Manikarnika Tambe in 1828 in Varanasi, and was fondly called "Manu" in her childhood. She grew up differently from most girls of her time, trained in horse riding, sword fighting, and archery alongside the boys in her father's household. This upbringing shaped the fearless warrior she would later become.

She was married to the Maharaja of Jhansi, Gangadhar Rao, and took the name Lakshmibai. When her husband passed away and the British East India Company refused to recognize her adopted son as the rightful heir under the Doctrine of Lapse, they moved to annex Jhansi. Lakshmibai refused to hand over her kingdom without resistance.

During the Revolt of 1857, she became one of its most prominent leaders. She organized and personally trained an army, including a regiment of women soldiers, and defended Jhansi fiercely against British forces. Even in retreat, she continued to fight, famously escaping on horseback with her son tied to her back.

She was killed in battle near Gwalior in 1858, fighting until her final moments. Today, she is remembered as one of India's greatest symbols of courage and resistance, a queen who chose to fight for her people's freedom rather than surrender.
